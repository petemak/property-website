# Brand Brief — Kigali Duplex Short-Stay

Source of truth: `business-context.md` (read in full). Where the prompt summary and the
file could differ, the file wins. Discrepancies are noted in Section 7.

---

## 1. Name and tagline

**Proposed name: Kigali Keys**

No brand exists yet, so this is a proposal for the next stations to accept or replace.
Rationale: "keys" carries a deliberate double meaning — the keys to a private, fully
furnished home *and* the keys to the Kia Sorento parked on site. That dual handover is
the single thing this stay offers that a comparable Kigali duplex does not, so the name
puts the differentiator at the centre of the brand rather than in the fine print. It is
short, easy to say for a non-English speaker, and works as a wordmark.

Alternatives if "Kigali Keys" is rejected: *Twin Hills Kigali*, *Umuryango House*
(Kinyarwanda, "the doorway / the household"), *Rebero Keys* (if the neighbourhood name
can be used).

**Tagline options (one line):**

- Primary: **"A furnished home in Kigali — with the keys to a car."**
- Alt 1: "Your Kigali address, and the freedom to drive it."
- Alt 2: "Settle in. Then go explore, on your own schedule."

## 2. Target audience

Three overlapping groups, all booking the whole home for short stays. First, international
**digital nomads and remote workers** (roughly 28–45, from Western Europe, North America
and increasingly other African hubs) who stay one to eight weeks, work from the property,
and place a high value on reliable wifi, a real desk, a kitchen, and the independence to
run errands and take weekend trips without negotiating taxis every day. Second,
**overseas leisure and business tourists** on shorter three-to-ten-night visits who want
a private, design-led alternative to a hotel and who see an on-site vehicle as a way to
reach Volcanoes National Park, Nyungwe or Lake Kivu without a separate rental agency.
Third, **Kigali-based urban professionals** needing a short local stay — relocation gaps,
home renovations, visiting family, a staycation. All three skew mobile-first, are
comfortable booking accommodation online, compare several listings before committing, and
read the practical details (price, what's included, how check-in works, how far things
are) closely before they trust a place. Assumed price sensitivity: mid-to-upper — they
will pay above a budget guesthouse for privacy, design and the car, but expect the
listing to justify it.

## 3. Voice

**Adjectives: warm, precise, confident, grounded.**

- **Do:** Give exact, useful specifics — travel times in minutes, prices in USD,
  bed sizes, what is and isn't included — the way Booking.com lays out a room, but in
  full sentences and a hospitable tone.
- **Don't:** Reach for travel-brochure superlatives or vague luxury language
  ("nestled in paradise", "unparalleled elegance"); if a claim can't be shown in a
  photo or a number, cut it.

## 4. Colour palette

| Role | Colour | Hex | Rationale |
|------|--------|-----|-----------|
| Primary | Hill Green | `#1F5B4E` | A deep, calm green for Rwanda's terraced hills. It reads as premium and trustworthy, and it deliberately steps away from both the Airbnb red and the wall of corporate blue used by Booking.com and Vrbo, so the single-property brand doesn't look like a clone of a platform. |
| Accent | Savanna Clay | `#D97A45` | A warm terracotta for calls to action, highlights and the "book" path. Supplies the modern, friendly warmth the owner likes in cozycozy, and signals hospitality and East African light without being literal. |
| Neutral anchor | Warm Charcoal | `#2B2A28` | Body text and UI framing. Softer than pure black, pairs with both primaries. |
| Background | Bone | `#FAF7F2` | Off-white canvas so photography carries the page, echoing Airbnb's clean, image-first layout. |

Use green for structure and trust, clay sparingly for action, and let large photos of the
house and the car do the emotional work against the bone background.

## 5. Typography direction

- **Headings: Fraunces** (Google Fonts). A warm, slightly characterful serif — gives the
  single property a boutique, hand-picked feel and distinguishes it from the geometric
  sans every booking platform uses. Use it for the wordmark, page titles and section
  headers.
- **Body: Inter** (Google Fonts). Screen-optimised, highly legible at small sizes and in
  dense information blocks (pricing tables, check-in steps, amenity lists), and renders
  cleanly for non-native English readers. Close in spirit to Airbnb's Cereal.

Both are free, well-supported and light to host, which suits the stated low, variable
budget. Fallback stack: `Georgia, serif` for headings, `system-ui, sans-serif` for body.

## 6. Reference sites — what to borrow, what to avoid

**Airbnb** (owner likes: sleek design)
- Borrow: large edge-to-edge photography, generous white space, a simple sticky
  date/price booking widget, clear amenity icons, mobile-first layout.
- Avoid: the signature red (ties the brand to a platform), review-wall clutter, and
  heavy trust-badge scaffolding a single owner doesn't need.

**Booking.com** (owner likes: compact information density)
- Borrow: scannable factual blocks, an explicit "what's included / what's not" section,
  a map with travel times to landmarks, clear price breakdown.
- Avoid: manufactured urgency and scarcity ("only 1 left!", "booked 5 times today"),
  cramped spacing, and the dominant blue.

**Vrbo** (owner likes: solid, serious feel)
- Borrow: whole-home framing, plain-spoken trustworthy tone, upfront house rules and
  cancellation terms.
- Avoid: dated stock-photo styling and corporate stiffness — this brand should feel
  personally hosted, not institutional.

**cozycozy.com** (owner likes: modern layout and colours) — noted in the file as
"Glovocozycozy.com"; interpreted as cozycozy.com.
- Borrow: the warm modern palette, rounded friendly UI, uncluttered comparison-style
  cards.
- Avoid: metasearch busyness and the low emotional attachment of an aggregator — a
  one-home site should feel specific and personal.

**Local reference — kigaliapartments.com / vibe.rw** (added for market context)
- Borrow: practical arrival and logistics guidance (airport transfer, SIM cards,
  neighbourhood orientation), local credibility.
- Avoid: generic real-estate-listing aesthetics, weak photography and rough mobile
  polish — the bar to clear locally is low, which is an opportunity.
